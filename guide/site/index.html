Mausritter Table Handbook

Every screen, button and tab in the app, with the Mausritter rule each one handles. Players can read sections 1–10. The GM should read everything.

1The table at a glance

Mausritter is a game about small, brave mice in a very big world. The GM describes a situation, each player says what their mouse does, and the GM says what happens. The rules only step in when something is risky.

The app gives that conversation a shared screen, laid out like a video game.

Top bar
Table code, sound, 3D dice, and who's online. See Taking a seat.
Stage
The shared screen. It shows scene art, the map, dice, fights and dialogue. Only the GM decides what goes on it.
Dice tray
Free dice under the stage, for any roll that isn't on a sheet.
Side panel
Players get My mouse, Party and Chronicle. The GM also gets Direct, Map and Fight.
How a session flows. The GM shows a scene and describes it. Players say what their mice try, and press a Save button when the GM asks for one. Time passes on the tracker, torches burn down, and the encounter die rolls. When a fight starts, the stage becomes the battle screen. Everything everyone does is written to the Chronicle.

3The stage GMPlayer

The stage is what the whole table looks at. The GM chooses what's on it, and everyone's dice and actions play out on it.

Party portraits
Top left: every mouse with an HP bar (green) and a STR bar (red). A portrait glows gold when the latest event is about that mouse, and flashes red when it's hurt.
Clock
Top right: day, watch and turn (e.g. "Day 1 · Morning watch · Turn 7/36"). The six dots fill toward the next light-usage mark, the footprint or cave icon shows wilderness or dungeon, and the last icon shows the season. See Time, light and encounters.
Result box
The latest roll: its dice (a struck-through die didn't count), the total in large type, what was rolled, and a stamped result: Success, Failed, Critical damage!, Slain, Level up!, Broken!, Encounter!, An omen…, All quiet, or the name of a new condition. It fades after a few seconds so the scene shows through again.
Speech box
When the GM speaks as a character, their portrait, name and line appear here, typing themselves out. Click the box to show the whole line at once.
Event ticker
Bottom left on wide screens: the last few things that happened. The full list is in the Chronicle tab.

4Dice and saves GMPlayer

The rule

When a mouse does something risky with an uncertain outcome, the GM asks for a save against STR (strength and toughness), DEX (speed and agility) or WIL (willpower and charm). Roll a d20: equal to or under the attribute succeeds, and over it fails.

  • Advantage (well prepared or well equipped): roll 2d20 and keep the lower one.
  • Disadvantage (badly placed or poorly equipped): roll 2d20 and keep the higher one.
  • Opposed saves: both sides roll, and the lowest success wins.
  • Clever plans don't need to roll. Dice are dangerous.
Save
Rolls a d20 against that attribute's current value. The dice land on the stage for everyone, and the verdict is stamped.
Adv · Normal · Disadv
Set this before pressing Save, when the GM grants advantage or imposes disadvantage.
Automatic disadvantage
The app adds disadvantage on its own: Injured affects STR and DEX, Drained affects WIL, and being Encumbered affects every save. A red note under the attribute says why. If advantage and disadvantage both apply, they cancel out (a ruling, see §15).

5Making a mouse Player

The rule
  • Attributes: for STR, DEX and WIL in order, roll 3d6 and keep the two highest (2–12). Then you may swap any two.
  • HP and pips: roll d6 for each. The pair picks your background from a 6×6 table, which gives two items (A and B).
  • Weak mice get help: if your highest attribute is 9 or less, roll a second background and take one of its items. At 7 or less, take both.
  • Kit: Torches, Rations, both background items, and a weapon of your choice.
  • Details: birthsign (with a disposition), coat colour and pattern, a physical detail (d66), and a mousy name.

Fight

The rule
  • Attacks always hit. Roll your weapon's die and deal that much, minus the target's armour.
  • Impaired attacks (firing into cover, while grappled) roll d4 whatever the weapon. Enhanced attacks (a clever gambit, a helpless target) roll d12.
  • Light weapons d6 · Medium d6 in one paw, d8 in both · Heavy d10 (both paws) · Light ranged d6 · Heavy ranged d8 · Improvised d6. With a weapon in each paw, roll both and keep the best.
  • Damage comes off HP first. Once HP is gone, it comes off STR, and the creature must make a STR save. Failing it is critical damage: the mouse becomes Injured and is incapacitated until an ally tends them and they take a short rest. STR 0 is death.
  • After a fight, roll d6 for each weapon, armour or ammunition that was used. On 4–6, mark a usage dot. Improvised weapons always mark.
⚔ Attack d6/d8
Rolls the dice of whatever is in your paws; the button shows which. In a fight it hits the foe you've targeted (see Combat).
Normal ▾
Normal, Impaired (d4) or Enhanced (d12).
dmg · Take damage
Applies damage from something the app isn't tracking, e.g. a fall. Armour, HP, STR, the critical save, Injured and death are all handled for you.
After the fight (n)
Appears once you've used weapons or armour. Rolls the usage check for each one.

7Inventory and conditions Player

The rule
  • Paw slots (main and off) hold what's ready to use. Body slots hold worn things, swapped to a paw as a free action. Pack slots take time: in a fight, digging something out of your pack is your action.
  • Heavy weapons, bows and armour take two slots. Light armour sits on the off paw and one body slot. Heavy armour takes both body slots.
  • Conditions take up slots too. Carry more than you have slots for and you're Encumbered: you can't run, and every save is at disadvantage.
  • Most items have three usage dots . When all are marked, the item is used up or broken.
  • Pips: the first 250 go in your pockets. Each further 250 needs a pip purse in a slot.
Drag a card
Move it to another slot. Two-slot cards fit side by side or one above the other, and the app finds the orientation that fits. Drop a card in Overflow to set it aside; anything there makes you Encumbered.
Usage dots
Click a dot to mark it, or click the last marked dot to clear it. A fully marked card is stamped Spent.
Card corner
Weapons show their die (d6/d8) and armour shows 1 def. Armour only protects when it's worn in its proper slots.
Grit tray
From level 2. Drag a condition here to ignore its effects and its slot. It stays there until cleared (see Levels).

8Rest, levels and hirelings Player

Rest

The rule
  • Short rest (1 turn): a swig of water, d6+1 HP back. Clears Frightened.
  • Long rest (1 watch): a meal and sleep restore all HP. If HP was already full, restore d6 to an attribute instead. Clears Exhausted (and Hungry, since a long rest includes a meal).
  • Full rest (a week in safety, about 20p of board): everything restored, and Injured and Drained cleared.
  • A mouse that doesn't rest for at least one watch a day becomes Exhausted.

The Short Long Full buttons under the inventory do all of this, and also end being incapacitated. Rests don't move the clock, so the GM should advance the turn tracker to match (1 turn, or 1 watch).

Pips, levels and Grit

The rule

You earn 1 XP per pip of treasure brought back to safety (split evenly across the party), and 1 XP per 10 pips spent selflessly on a community.

Banked items and pips

Hirelings

The rule

A typical hireling has d6 HP, 2d6 for each attribute, and six slots (two paws and four pack slots in this app). Wages per day: torchbearer 1p, labourer 2p, tunnel digger 5p, armourer 8p, guide 10p, mouse-at-arms 10p, scholar 20p, knight 25p, interpreter 30p. When stressed, unpaid or asked to do something too dangerous, a hireling must pass a WIL save or flee.

Hire rolls up a hireling of the chosen type. Click one to open its own small sheet with saves, attack, damage, inventory and rest, then Dismiss to let it go. Hirelings take part in fights and act in turn order.

At the bottom of the sheet, Retire this mouse removes it from the table. When a mouse dies, the button reads Lay to rest & roll a new mouse. The rules want you back in the game quickly, so use + New mouse at the top of the tab right away.

9Spells Player

The rule
  • Spells are spirits trapped in carved tablets. To cast one, hold it in a paw and read it aloud.
  • Choose a power up to the tablet's remaining usage dots, and roll that many d6. Each die showing 4–6 marks a usage dot. The effect uses [SUM] (the total) and [DICE] (how many dice).
  • Miscast: each 6 rolled deals d6 WIL damage to the caster. Then make a WIL save; fail it and you're Drained.
  • A spell with all its dots marked is depleted until its recharge condition is met (e.g. "bury in a peaceful field for three days"). A charged spell sells for d6×100p.

Put the tablet in a paw slot, click it, and choose 1 die 2 dice… The app rolls, marks usage, applies any miscast and the Drained save, and shows the effect text with the numbers filled in (e.g. "Deal 11 + 3 damage to a creature within sight"). The card menu also shows the spell's recharge.

11Time, light and encounters GM

The rule
UnitLengthUsed when
Roundunder a minuteFighting
Turnabout 10 minutesExploring a dangerous place. One major action (search a room, pick a lock, a fight) is one turn.
Watch36 turns, about 6 hoursTravel and rest. A day has four: Night, Morning (from sunrise), Afternoon (from noon), Evening (from sunset).
  • Light: a lit torch or lantern marks a usage dot every 6 turns. Electric lanterns have six dots instead of three.
  • The encounter die (d6): 1 is an encounter, 2 is an omen (signs of one), 3–6 is all quiet. Roll it every 3 turns in a dungeon (or after loud mayhem), and at the start of the Morning and Evening watches in the wilderness, where a d12 picks the hour.
  • Travel: one one-mile hex per watch, or two watches through difficult terrain (streams, human roads, big rocks, hills).

The tracker lives in the GM's Direct tab, and the result shows on everyone's clock. The app does the bookkeeping:

  • Every +1 turn, +1 hour (6 turns) or +1 watch burns lit lights on every mouse and hireling and logs any that go out.
  • In Dungeon mode, the encounter die rolls on every third turn. Advancing a whole watch rolls all twelve, and the stage shows the worst result.
  • In Wilderness mode, it rolls whenever time passes sunrise or sunset, with the d12 hour.
  • The stage darkens at night and turns amber in the evening.
Encounter tables. On an encounter or omen, roll on your own d6 table for that area. The rulebook suggests entries 1–3 for a common encounter, 4–5 for an unusual one, and 6 for something dangerous or weird.

13GM: the hexcrawl map GMPlayer

The rule
  • Start with a 5×5 map of one-mile hexes and a friendly mouse settlement in the centre.
  • For each hex, roll a type (d6: 1–2 countryside, 3–4 forest, 5 river, 6 human town), a landmark from that type's d20 table, and a detail (d6 then d8), e.g. "Rat bandit hideout (Who do they prey on?)".
  • Detail two to four landmarks as adventure sites, spread out so the mice criss-cross the map. Seed a d6 rumour table: 1–3 true, 4–5 partly true, 6 false.
  • When the mice first leave an adventure site, show them a blank map with only the settlement, the site and a couple of well-known places. They find the rest by travelling, and spot obvious landmarks automatically as they pass through.

Shown when the map is empty. Generate rolls every hex from the rulebook tables at the size you pick (up to 15×15); Blank gives plain countryside to paint yourself.

14Combat GMPlayer

The rule
  • Who goes first: if the mice attack unsuspecting foes, they act first. Otherwise each mouse makes a DEX save: pass and you act before the foes, fail and you act after. That order holds for the rest of the fight. Frogs always go first unless surprised.
  • On your turn: move up to 12″ and take one action: attack, negotiate, flee, or a risky gambit such as tripping or disarming (usually a save).
  • Attacks always hit, and damage works as described in Fight. Some creatures are different: a spider's or centipede's bite damages DEX, a ghost's touch damages WIL, and many have their own critical effect in place of Injured ("carried away in web", "swallowed whole").
  • Morale: an enemy makes a WIL save, and flees or surrenders if it fails, when it is clearly outmatched at the start, takes critical damage for the first time, or sees an ally fall or flee.

15Rulings and what's missing

Rulings this table uses

The rulebook leaves these open, so the app picks one way, agreed for this group:

  • Advantage and disadvantage on the same save cancel out to a normal roll.
  • A long rest also clears Hungry, since it includes a meal.
  • Hirelings have two paw slots and four pack slots, as on the hireling sheet.
  • A long rest at full HP restores d6 to the most-damaged attribute.
  • A foe tests morale for "critical damage" the first time it takes STR damage and stays standing (failing the STR save already takes it out of the fight).

Not in the app yet

  • Weather by season (the 2d6 table) and its Exhausted saves for travel. Roll 2d6 in the dice tray for now.
  • Generators for settlements, NPCs, adventure sites and treasure; factions; a quest journal.
  • Warbands and their battles.
  • Automatic Exhausted for a day without rest, and Hungry for a day without a ration. Add these from + Add → Condition.
